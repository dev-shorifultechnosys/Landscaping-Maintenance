A strong landscape design connects how people move, where they gather, what needs screening, how water is managed and how much care the garden should need.

Dawson develops the direction around the property rather than applying a standard style. The process can help clarify a complete new landscape, a staged plan or the most useful improvements within an existing yard.

PurposeSpaces aligned with everyday use and long-term priorities.
ProportionPlanting, paths, lawns and built elements balanced together.

Design scope

What the plan can address.

The right level of detail depends on the site and project. These are the core decisions typically explored.

01

Site & priorities

Access, orientation, existing elements, problem areas, views and the way the property needs to work.

02

Layout & flow

Clear relationships between entries, entertaining areas, paths, lawns, planting and service zones.

03

Materials

A coordinated direction for paving, limestone, edging, retaining and other structural elements.

04

Planting direction

Form, seasonal interest, screening, shade and water needs considered for Perth conditions.

05

Water & levels

Drainage, irrigation thinking and level changes considered alongside the layout.

06

Staging & budget

Priorities organised into a realistic scope or staged pathway when everything cannot happen at once.

Designed for Perth

Character without
unnecessary upkeep.

A garden can feel rich and established while still responding sensibly to sun, sandy soils, water use and the amount of maintenance you want to take on.

  • Waterwise and climate-aware plant direction
  • Shade, screening and seasonal change considered
  • Plant scale matched to available space
  • Maintenance expectations discussed before selection

Design outputs

Know what the direction resolves.

The exact documentation must be confirmed for each project. This framework shows the decisions a useful design conversation should bring together.

Potential project outputs

  • Site priorities and functional layout direction
  • Material and hardscape character
  • Planting form, screening and seasonal intent
  • Irrigation and maintenance considerations
  • Construction scope or staged project pathway

Final deliverables depend on the agreed design service and should be listed clearly in the proposal.

Design process

From brief to direction.

A clear brief makes the design more useful and helps reduce avoidable changes later.

01

Project brief

Share priorities, inspiration, constraints, photos, timing and a realistic budget range.

02

Site review

Understand access, existing conditions, levels, orientation and what should remain or change.

03

Design direction

Develop the overall layout, material approach, planting character and project priorities.

04

Build pathway

Clarify the next scope, whether delivered together or organised into sensible stages.
